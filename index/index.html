The History of Writing Materials

[1] Long before the invention of paper, ancient civilizations recorded their knowledge using a variety of surfaces. In ancient Mesopotamia, scribes used reed styluses to press wedge-shaped marks into wet clay tablets, which were later baked in the sun or kilns to ensure permanence.

[2] Meanwhile, ancient Egyptians revolutionized record-keeping with the creation of papyrus around 3000 BCE. Made from the pith of the papyrus plant native to the Nile delta, these scrolls provided a lightweight, portable medium that could be easily rolled and transported across the Mediterranean.

[3] Centuries later, parchment and vellum—derived from treated animal skins—became prominent in Europe, particularly during the Middle Ages. These materials were remarkably durable, allowing medieval monks to produce lavishly illustrated manuscripts that survived for over a thousand years.
